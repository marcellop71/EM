\subsection{The Barriers}
\label{sec:four-way}
%% =========================================================================

Before cataloguing what fails, it is worth stating precisely what has to
succeed.  For every missing prime~$q$ we need one of these equivalent
statements:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{DH}: If the multipliers generate $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$,
  the walk $\walkZ{2}{q}{n} = \Prod{2}(n) \bmod q$ hits $-1$ cofinally.
\item \textbf{CCSB}: For every non-trivial character
  $\chi \colon (\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$, the sum
  $\sum_{n < N} \chi(\walkZ{2}{q}{n}) = o(N)$.
\item \textbf{$d{=}2$ special case} (as a stepping stone): For every
  quadratic character~$\chi$, the $\pm 1$-valued walk character sum is
  $o(N)$.
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{The Four-Way Blocker.}
The majority of the \DEentries~dead ends reduce to a single meta-obstacle:
every known technique for proving equidistribution of sequences on
finite groups requires at least \emph{one} of (1)~independence of
steps, (2)~multiplicativity of the generating function,
(3)~algebraic-geometric structure (parameter families, monodromy),
or (4)~ergodic stationarity.  The EM walk satisfies \emph{none} of
these: the steps are deterministic and mutually dependent, the walk
is not a multiplicative function, the multipliers have no known
algebraic-geometric parametrization, and the non-autonomous dynamics
(a different multiplier at each step) rule out stationarity.  This
explains why classical tools---random-walk mixing, Hal\'asz's theorem,
Katz monodromy, Birkhoff averages---all fail.


\paragraph{The telescope exhausts the algebra (a taxonomy, not a theorem).}
The telescoping identity $\chi(w(n{+}1)) = \chi(w(n)) \cdot
\chi(m(n))$ is the algebraic content of the walk we know how to use.
The two decomposition strategies for the character sum $S_N =
\sum_{n<N} \chi(w(n))$ that we have found productive are grouping by
\emph{value} (fiber decomposition $\to$ CME) and grouping by \emph{lag}
(autocorrelation $\to$ HOD).  Every other rearrangement we tried---Abel
summation, M\"obius inversion, Dirichlet series, block
decomposition---either reduced to one of these two or failed outright
(Abel gives $O(N^2)$ remainder in the wrong direction; M\"obius/Dirichlet
require multiplicativity; Dead Ends~\#28, \#38, \#103).  We record this as
an empirical taxonomy; it is not a theorem that no third route exists.

\smallskip\noindent
The pattern: every approach that avoids the specific EM orbit's
joint distribution either reduces to CCSB or fails.  Since CME
implies CCSB (proved), and CME decomposes as VCB~$+$~Dec
(\S\ref{sec:vcb}), the sharpest targets are now VCB~$+$~PED or CME:
conditional equidistribution of multipliers given walk position, or
the weaker proportionality condition combined with escape density.
CME implies CCSB, and both are equivalent to MC as propositions (they
are quantified over missing primes; \lean{EM/CME/Equivalences.lean}{cme\_iff\_mc}):
CME is the \emph{irreducible analytic reformulation}, not a weaker
hypothesis.


\paragraph{The two fundamental barriers, witnessed.}
\label{sec:barriers-witnessed}
Dead Ends~\#90 and~\#117 are the two entries of this section that admit
finite counterexamples rather than surveyed failures.  We should be
candid about their weight: they are \emph{illustrations} that first-order
multiplier statistics do not determine hitting and that multiplier
cancellation does not force walk cancellation---elementary facts about
ordering, which is exactly why every live hypothesis is
\emph{conditional} on the walk position---not theorems that block any
technique.  Both are machine-checked, by two periodic multiplier
sequences in $(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$---cyclic of order~$4$, generated
by~$2$---that differ only by a rearrangement:
\[
  A = (2,2,3,3)^\infty,
  \qquad
  B = (2,3,2,3)^\infty .
\]
Their walks are $1,2,4,2,1,\dots$ and $1,2,1,2,\dots$ respectively, so
$A$ reaches the death class $-1 = 4$ and $B$ provably never does.  But
in \emph{every} window of four consecutive steps both sequences use the
multiplier~$2$ exactly twice and~$3$ exactly twice: they have identical
empirical multiplier distributions.

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{Population statistics do not determine orbit hitting}
(\lean{EM/Meta/OrbitBarrier.lean}{population\_does\_not\_determine\_hitting}).
Two prime-valued multiplier sequences with the same empirical
distribution in every window, one of whose walks hits the death class
and one of whose walks provably never does.  Since
$\walkZ{2}{q}{n} = -1 \iff q \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1$ and cofinal hitting
of~$-1$ is equivalent to MC at~$q$, this is Dead End~\#90 as a theorem:
\emph{no statistic of the multiplier population carries information
about whether the orbit hits}.  It is precisely the gap that every
population-to-orbit transfer hypothesis has to cross---and, since
the population statements themselves turned out to be false (Dead
End~\#160), the gap now stands undisguised as CME\@.

\smallskip
\textbf{Multiplier cancellation does not force walk cancellation}
(\lean{EM/Meta/OrbitBarrier.lean}{mult\_cancel\_not\_walk\_cancel}).
For the same sequence~$B$ and the order-4 character with
$\chi(2) = i$---so $\chi(3) = i^3 = -i$---the multiplier sums alternate
$i, -i$ and stay bounded by~$1$ for all~$K$, while the walk alternates
$1,2$ and its character sums are $M(1+i)$ after $2M$ steps, hence
$\Theta(K)$.  This is Dead End~\#117.  The character carries exactly the
side conditions the formalization's own MultCancelToWalkCancel
hypothesis imposes ($\|\chi\| \leq 1$, $\chi(0) = 0$, orthogonality), so
the counterexample is not an artifact of a weak statement.
\end{keyresult}

\noindent
The same sequence witnesses a third entry.  Its multipliers $\{2,3\}$
\emph{generate} $(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$
(\lean{EM/Meta/OrbitBarrier.lean}{multipliers\_generate}) while the walk
visits only $\{1,2\}$: generation is not coverage, so SubgroupEscape---
proved and free (\S\ref{sec:walk})---cannot close the gap.  This is
Dead Ends~\#20 and~\#130 in multiplicative form.

All of it is assembled, together with the proved sufficient reductions
($\mathrm{HH} \Rightarrow \MC$, $\mathrm{DH} \Rightarrow \MC$,
$\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \MC$) and the two proved structural inputs one
gets for free (the death-class characterization and PrimeResidueEscape),
as \lean{EM/Meta/OrbitBarrier.lean}{integer\_orbit\_barrier\_thesis}---the
integer analogue of the function-field
\lean{EM/FunctionField/OrbitBarrier.lean}{orbit\_barrier\_thesis}
of~\S\ref{sec:function-field}.  The gap between what is free and what is
witnessed to be unreachable \emph{is} the orbit-specificity barrier, and
every open hypothesis in the reduction network lives inside it.


\paragraph{The sure layer cannot constrain one orbit.}
The seed-average programme of \S\ref{sec:seed-average} contains exactly
one ingredient that is valid along a single orbit: its \emph{sure} layer
--- statements that are per-path and hypothesis-free, of which the
harmonic charge budget
(\lean{EM/Population/LargeStepRoughness.lean}{charge\_sum\_le\_harmonic})
is the strongest.  Writing $B_r(k)$ for the box of unit residues mod~$r$
not yet excluded by an exposed step, and calling a step \emph{charged}
when $r$ is still active, the step is $r$-exposed, and the cofactor
residue mod~$r$ is new, it reads
$\sum_{k<n,\ \mathrm{charged}} 1/\#B_r(k) \le H_{r-1}$.  It nevertheless
carries \emph{no} information about the set of primes missed by one
orbit, because it is an identity plus a pigeonhole.  The box starts at
$\#B_r(0) = r-1$
(\lean{EM/Population/LargeStepRoughness.lean}{boxCard\_zero}); a charge
decrements it by \emph{exactly} one
(\lean{EM/Population/LargeStepRoughness.lean}{boxCard\_succ\_of\_charged})
and a non-charge leaves it unchanged
(\lean{EM/Population/LargeStepRoughness.lean}{boxCard\_of\_not\_charged}).
So with $C$ charges before~$n$ the left-hand side telescopes to
$H_{r-1} - H_{r-1-C}$, and the only content left in the inequality is that
$C \leq r-2$, i.e.\ that every term $1/\#B_r(k)$ is well defined --- which
counting the units mod~$r$ forces anyway.  Zero orbit-distinguishing
content, at every~$r$ and every horizon.

There is also a model obstruction, and it is the cleanest of the six.
Every theorem of the layer is proved about the \emph{$q$-free} dynamics
(\S\ref{sec:seed-average}), and that dynamics provably never selects~$q$
(\lean{EM/Population/SeedCapture.lean}{genSeqAvoid\_ne\_avoided}); the
whole layer is therefore satisfied by a process that misses~$q$ by
construction, so no consequence of it alone can force a capture.  Dead
Ends~\#169--\#174 record the six independent obstructions: the identity
collapse~(\#169), the surviving ``the box has not collapsed'' branch,
which unwinds to $\neg\DH(r)$ and is the mirror of the circularity
in~\#166~(\#170), the model obstruction~(\#171, with a formal Lean
witness), the wrong per-path sign of the compensator, maximal exactly for
orbits that capture nothing~(\#172), its inapplicability to the true
orbit, whose multipliers admit no sure size bound~(\#173), and the
$(\mathrm{C}\infty)$ gate on any bound for the missed-prime counting
function~(\#174).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The sign asymmetry of $\minFac$.}\enspace
Behind all of this is one structural fact, worth stating on its own.  The
definition $\seq{2}(n) = \minFac(\Prod{2}(n)+1)$ yields infinitely many
\emph{negative} facts --- $r \nmid \Prod{2}(n)+1$ for every prime
$r < \seq{2}(n)$ --- and exactly \emph{one} positive fact, about a prime
that is captured by definition.  Every sure ingredient \emph{in this programme} is
a deduction from these together with multiplier distinctness, hence every
one of them is a non-divisibility statement about primes that are
\emph{not} captured.  So this layer produces only \emph{upper} bounds on
hit counts --- indeed $\#\{n : q \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1\} \leq \pi(q)$ for every
prime~$q$, missed or captured --- and never the lower bound of~$1$ that
capture requires.  This is a screening test for any per-path proposal:
\emph{does it produce a positive divisibility fact about a prescribed
prime?}  If not, it is inert, however unconditional it looks.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The $\FF_5[t]$ litmus test.}\enspace
The function-field refutation (Theorem~\ref{thm:ffmc-false-five}) turns
the screening test into a theorem about proof strategies.  Over
$\FF_5[t]$ the greedy dynamics has every structural feature the integer
proofs use---a residue walk in a cyclic group at each irreducible, the
coprimality cascade, distinct irreducible multipliers, the exact
telescope---and Mullin's conjecture is \emph{false}, because every
Euclid polynomial of the seed-$t$ sequence is irreducible.  Hence any
argument for $\MC$ that used only walk and residue data would transplant
to $\FF_5[t]$ and prove a falsehood.  A proof must use what $\FF_5[t]$
lacks, and the only structural difference on file is that integer Euclid
numbers are composite infinitely often: the proof must be
\emph{anatomical}, in the precise sense of using $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$-type
input about the factorization of the Euclid numbers themselves.  This is
the converse of the min/max dichotomy's lesson that any disproof must be
anatomical (\S\ref{sec:min-max-dichotomy}); together they say that the residue
layer alone can neither prove nor refute the conjecture.  Dead
End~\#90's witness, two artificial multiplier sequences in
$(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$, is thereby replaced by a natural one.  The test is
usable in advance: a proposed intermediate hypothesis about the walk that
would still hold over $\FF_5[t]$ cannot suffice over~$\ZZ$.

\smallskip\noindent
What is machine-checked is much narrower than the four-way blocker: two

(\S\ref{sec:barriers-witnessed}).  The blocker itself is a taxonomy of

(\S\ref{sec:ensemble}) bypasses this, but introduces a new gap:

subject of the next subsection.

\subsection{What the Walk Has That Nothing Exploits}
\label{sec:walk-features}
%% =========================================================================

\paragraph{Structural Features of the EM Walk}

The dead ends above show what does not work.  Complementarily, the EM
walk has four structural features---all proved or formalized---that no
dead-end approach has successfully exploited.  Any proof of MC will
almost certainly use at least one.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Feature~1: Super-exponential growth.}\enspace
$\Prod{2}(n) \geq 2^n$
(\lean{EM/LargeSieve/Structural.lean}{prod\_lower\_bound\_for\_sieve}).  The
Euclid numbers $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ grow absurdly fast.  This means the
\emph{sieve level}---the threshold below which all prime factors have
been excluded---grows super-exponentially.  By step~$n$, the Euclid
number $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ is coprime to each of $\seq{2}(0), \ldots, \seq{2}(n)$,
a growing set of distinct primes.  The pool of ``available'' small
primes as factors of $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ shrinks, but the size of
$\Prod{2}(n)+1$ grows so fast that it must have enormous prime factors
most of the time.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Feature~2: Mutual coprimality of Euclid
numbers.}\enspace For $m > n$, $\Prod{2}(m)$ is divisible by
$\seq{2}(n+1)$, which divides $\Prod{2}(n)+1$.  So
$\Prod{2}(m)+1 \equiv 1 \pmod{\seq{2}(n+1)}$: successive Euclid numbers
live in different residue classes modulo earlier sequence terms.  This
coprimality structure means the Euclid numbers cannot all ``avoid'' a
residue class in a coordinated way---their residues are forced apart
by the construction.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Feature~3: The multiplier is the smallest
prime factor.}\enspace  This is the key constraint that everyone
mentions but nobody has quantified.  If $\walkZ{2}{q}{n} \neq -1$ (so
that $q$ does not divide $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ as the smallest factor), then
the multiplier $\multZ{2}{q}{n} = \minFac(\Prod{2}(n)+1)$ satisfies
$\multZ{2}{q}{n} \leq (\Prod{2}(n)+1)^{1/2}$.  For a number of size
${\sim}2^n$, this smallest factor could be as small as~$3$ or as
large as~${\sim}2^{n/2}$.  The $\minFac$ rule creates a deterministic
coupling between walk position and multiplier: the multiplier at
step~$n$ depends on the full value of~$\Prod{2}(n)+1$, not just its
residue.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Feature~4: Self-correcting feedback.}\enspace
If the walk concentrates on certain residues mod~$q$---say
$\walkZ{2}{q}{n} \equiv a \pmod{q}$ for many~$n$---then
$\Prod{2}(n)+1 \equiv a+1 \pmod{q}$ for many~$n$.  The smallest prime
factor of numbers $\equiv a+1 \pmod{q}$ depends on~$a+1$, creating a
feedback loop: concentration in one residue class biases the
multiplier distribution, which in turn pushes the walk away from that
class.  This mechanism is formalized as the Lyapunov drift analysis
of \S\ref{sec:self-correcting}: the visit-count Lyapunov function
telescopes to the cumulative drift, sub-quadratic drift (SCD) implies
VE and hence MC, and even a one-sided linear drift bound (LinearDrift)
suffices via a threshold-free route.  The zero-bias property under
uniform multipliers is proved; the orbit-specificity gap---does the
EM orbit satisfy the drift bound?---remains open
(Section~\ref{sec:spectral-routes}).

\medskip
These four features---growth, coprimality, the $\minFac$ selection
rule, and self-correcting feedback---are the raw material that any
successful approach must engage with.  The dead ends above fail
precisely because they treat the walk generically (as a random walk,
or as an arbitrary multiplicative walk) rather than exploiting the
specific arithmetic of the EM construction.


\subsection{The Selectability Perspective}
\label{sec:selectability}

The Euclid construction guarantees fresh primes at every step: every
prime factor of~$\Prod{2}(n)+1$ is new (coprime to the running product).
The difficulty of MC is not the \emph{existence} of new primes but
whether the $\minFac$ rule eventually \emph{selects} each one.  The
formalization makes this contrast precise.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}{divisor\_not\_yet\_in\_seq}]
\label{thm:divisor-fresh}
If $p \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1$, then $\seq{2}(m) \neq p$ for all $m \leq n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Any $\seq{2}(m)$ with $m \leq n$ divides $\Prod{2}(n)$.  A number $\geq 2$
cannot divide both~$a$ and~$a+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}{passed\_over\_persists}]
If $p \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1$ but $\seq{2}(n\!+\!1) \neq p$ (the $\minFac$ rule
chose a smaller prime), then $\seq{2}(m) \neq p$ for all $m \leq n+1$.
The prime survives to potentially divide future Euclid numbers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}{selectability\_extinguished}]
\label{thm:extinct}
Once $\seq{2}(m) = p$, we have $p \mid \Prod{2}(n)$ for all $n \geq m$, so
$p \nmid \Prod{2}(n)+1$ ever again.  Selectability is a one-shot resource.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[\defname{InfinitelySelectable}]\label{def:inf-sel}
A prime~$p$ is \emph{infinitely selectable} if $p \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1$ for
cofinally many~$n$: $\forall\, N,\, \exists\, n \geq N,\,
p \mid \Prod{2}(n)+1$.
\end{definition}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:extinct}, MC$(p)$ and
Infinitely\-Selectable$(p)$ are mutually exclusive
(\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}%
{mc\_implies\_not\_infinitely\_selectable}):
a prime that enters the sequence can never be selectable again.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}{dh\_implies\_infinitely\_selectable}]
\label{thm:dh-inf-sel}
Under DH, every prime that never appears in the sequence (with SE
satisfied) is infinitely selectable.
\end{theorem}

The comparison between the $\minFac$ rule and alternative selection
rules---random factor, $\{\minFac, \operatorname{secondMinFac}\}$,
and the function field analog---reveals why independence is the key
missing ingredient.  Section~\ref{sec:variants} develops these
variants in detail; the core observation is that the random variant
possesses \textbf{independence of multiplier from walk position}
(the sole property needed for mixing), while $\minFac$ trades
independence for perfect selection efficiency.  Stochastic domination
fails because the two processes diverge after one step
(\S\ref{sec:stoch-dom}).

\paragraph{The Marginal/Joint Barrier}
\label{sec:oracle}

The verified reductions (TailSE, CofinalEscape, QuotientDH) exhaust
what can be proved about the \emph{marginal} distribution of multiplier
residues.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Equidist/OrbitAnalysis.lean}{emfe\_iff\_tail\_se\_at}]
$\mathrm{EuclidMinFacEscape}(q) \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{TailSE}(q)$.
\end{theorem}

Even perfect per-position equidistribution of multipliers is consistent
with HH failure.  DH is a \emph{joint} statement---the
(position, multiplier) pair must hit the \emph{death curve}
$\multZ{2}{q}{n} = -\walkZ{2}{q}{n}^{-1}$---and no marginal statement can
force this.

\paragraph{The orbit chain gap.}
The cofinal orbit analysis picks one cofinal multiplier~$s_x$ per
walk position, producing a cycle $x_0 \to x_1 \to \cdots \to x_0$
in $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$.  Even when the cofinal multipliers generate
the full group, the cycle size~$k$ can be less than~$|(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times|$.
Example: in $\ZZ/6\ZZ$, the cycle $0 \to 1 \to 0$ has
$\langle 1, 5 \rangle = \ZZ/6\ZZ$ but misses~3.

Closing this gap requires showing that at each cofinally visited
position, \emph{multiple} multiplier classes appear---expanding the
cycle until it covers $-1$.  This is the ``specific-orbit problem'':
transferring generic equidistribution of $\minFac$ residues to the
particular EM orbit.


\subsection{The BRE Impossibility for $d \geq 3$}
\label{sec:bre-impossibility}

\begin{deadend}[title={Dead End: PED $\not\Rightarrow$ CCSB for $d \geq 3$}]
\begin{remark}\label{rem:bre-impossible}
Positive escape density (PED) alone does \emph{not} imply
CCSB for characters of order $d \geq 3$.

\emph{Counterexample}: a walk on $\ZZ/3\ZZ$ that alternates between
only two of the three $d$-th roots of unity (phase-aligned escapes)
achieves positive escape density yet has walk sum
$\approx N/2 \cdot (1 + \omega) \neq o(N)$.

For $d = 2$ this degeneracy vanishes: the only non-trivial rotation
is~$-1$, so escape frequency \emph{is} the rotation distribution.
The order-2 BRE from NoLongRuns$(L)$ is proved in the formalization
(\lean{EM/Equidist/SelfCorrecting.lean}{order2\_noLongRuns\_mc}).
But for $d \geq 3$, PED constrains how often the walk rotates without
constraining the \emph{distribution} among $d-1$ non-identity
rotations.  The $\mathrm{PED} \Rightarrow \mathrm{BRE} \Rightarrow
\mathrm{CCSB}$ factorization is invalid for $d \geq 3$.

This barrier is specific to the PED route.  The CME $\to$ CCSB
reduction (\lean{EM/CME/Reduction.lean}{cme\_implies\_ccsb})
bypasses PED and BRE entirely, working for all character orders $d$
via the telescoping identity.  The $d \geq 3$ problem is therefore
not a barrier for the \emph{reduction}---only for the particular
factorization through PED.
\end{remark}
\end{deadend}

\paragraph{The Van der Corput Barrier}

The van der Corput inequality (Theorem~\ref{thm:vdc}, proved in the
formalization) converts character sum bounds into autocorrelation
bounds.  Theorem~\ref{thm:shift-one} gives $R_1 = o(N)$ under the
Decorrelation Hypothesis.  A single shift is not enough: in the classical
form, $H = 1$ yields
$|S_N|^2 \leq \frac{N+1}{2}(N + 2|R_1|) = N^2/2 + o(N^2)$, hence
$|S_N| \leq N/\sqrt{2}$ --- non-trivial, but a constant fraction of the
trivial bound and \emph{not} $o(N)$; in the weakened form actually proved
here the right-hand side $2N^2/(H+1) + 2\delta N^2$ is $o(N^2)$ only when
$H \to \infty$ \emph{and} $\delta \to 0$, which makes the same point
structurally.  To get $o(N)$ one needs higher-order correlations
$R_h = o(N)$ for all $h \leq H$ with $H \to \infty$, which is
HigherOrderDecorrelation (Theorem~\ref{thm:hod-mc}).
The telescoping identity $\sum_n \chi(w(n))(\chi(m(n))-1) = O(1)$
is a precise structural constraint.

\paragraph{The Walk Bridge Falsity}
\label{sec:walk-bridge-false}

The BV route decomposes into two stages: sieve transfer
(BV~$\Rightarrow$~EMMultCSB, bounding \emph{multiplier} character sums)
and the walk bridge (EMMultCSB~$\Rightarrow$~MMCSB, converting multiplier
bounds to \emph{walk} bounds).  The walk bridge
\textbf{MultCSBImpliesMMCSB}
(\lean{EM/LargeSieve/Structural.lean}{MultCSBImpliesMMCSB})
is stated as an open \code{Prop} and is \textbf{false in general}.

The obstruction is structural: the walk character sum is a
\emph{partial product} $\chi(w(n)) = \prod_{k<n} \chi(m(k))$
of the multiplier characters.  Even when the individual factors
$\chi(m(k))$ are equidistributed on the unit circle (so their
\emph{sum} cancels), their \emph{partial products} perform a
multiplicative walk whose norm grows as~$\sqrt{N}$,
not~$o(N)$.  Cancellation of sums does not imply cancellation
of cumulative products.

This negative result explains why the CME bypass
(Definition~\ref{def:cme}) is essential.  CME uses fiber
decomposition and telescoping to go directly from conditional
multiplier equidistribution to CCSB, circumventing the walk bridge
entirely.


\subsection{The Factorization Independence Heuristic}
\label{sec:hash-heuristic}

The preceding barriers explain why MC is hard to \emph{prove}.  This
subsection explains why it should be \emph{true}---and why the
formalization's sole remaining hypothesis (CME) is the precise
mathematical content of a natural intuition about factorization.

\paragraph{The information bottleneck.}
The information bottleneck of \S\ref{sec:sufficient-conditions}---$O(\log q)$
bits visible to the walk versus ${\sim}2^n$ bits determining the
multiplier---means the mutual information vanishes exponentially.
This is directly analogous to the pseudorandomness of iterated hash
functions: if~$H$ is a cryptographic hash, the sequence
$x, H(x), H(H(x)), \ldots$ is deterministic but statistically
indistinguishable from random, because the hash destroys recoverable
correlations.  For the EM sequence, integer factorization plays the
role of the hash function.  Both processes are fully deterministic
(given the seed, every term is uniquely determined), one-way
(computing forward is trivial; extracting structure from the output
is computationally hard), and \emph{de facto} uncorrelated
(consecutive terms pass every reasonable test for independence).

The analogy is heuristic, not rigorous.  SHA-256 is \emph{designed}
for pseudorandomness; $\minFac$ is not designed for anything.  We do
not claim that computational hardness of factoring implies MC---what
MC requires is a number-theoretic statement (CME), not a
complexity-theoretic one.  But the analogy explains the structure of
the problem: proving pseudorandomness of a deterministic process
requires showing that \emph{no exploitable structure exists}, which is
harder than finding structure.  This is why the conjecture resists
proof despite overwhelming heuristic evidence.

\paragraph{Negative analogies: why the selection rule matters.}
The factorization independence heuristic explains why the EM sequence
($\minFac$ variant) should contain all primes, while related sequences
do not.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The \textbf{Sylvester sequence} $s(n{+}1) = s(0) \cdots s(n) + 1$
  has density-zero prime divisors~\cite{Odoni1985}.  Its terms grow
  doubly exponentially, giving each prime only $O(1)$ chances to appear
  as a factor.  The ``hash chain'' runs too fast.

\item \textbf{Fermat numbers} $F_n = 2^{2^n} + 1$ have the even stronger
  property that $\sum 1/p$ converges over their prime divisors.  Again,
  doubly exponential growth limits opportunities.

\item \textbf{The $\FF_5[t]$ sequence} is the Sylvester case realised:
  every Euclid polynomial of the seed-$t$ sequence is irreducible
  (\lean{EM/FunctionField/StableTower.lean}{tower\_euclid\_irreducible}),
  so the ``hash chain'' never compresses and only one irreducible of each
  degree $2^k$ is ever selected---the function-field conjecture is
  \emph{false} there (Theorem~\ref{thm:ffmc-false-five}).

\item The \textbf{second EM sequence} ($\mathrm{maxFac}$ variant)
  provably omits infinitely many primes~\cite{CoxVdP1968,
  PollackTrevino2014}.  Here the ``hash function'' ($\mathrm{maxFac}$
  instead of $\minFac$) produces large multipliers, causing the product
  to grow rapidly---analogous to using a hash function that amplifies
  rather than compresses.

\item The \textbf{first EM sequence} ($\minFac$ variant) keeps the
  product growing slowly---heuristically as $\exp(n^2/2)$
  (see~\S\ref{sec:lean}).  This is analogous to a hash function that
  compresses, giving exponentially many iterations within any fixed
  modulus.  The conjecture is that this compression ensures coverage.
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{What the formalization adds.}
The formalization identifies three equivalent formulations of the
factoring channel's decorrelation:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Conditional Multiplier Equidistribution (CME)}:
  the distribution of $\minFac(\Prod{2}(n)+1) \bmod q$, conditioned on
  $\Prod{2}(n) \equiv c \pmod{q}$, is asymptotically independent of~$c$
  (Definition~\ref{def:cme}).
\item \textbf{Decorrelation Hypothesis}: the character sum
  $\sum \chi(\multZ{2}{q}{n}) = o(N)$ for nontrivial~$\chi$---the
  multiplier residues have cancelling character sums, as independent
  random variables would.
\item \textbf{ComplexCharSumBound (CCSB)}: the walk character sums
  $\sum \chi(\walkZ{2}{q}{n}) = o(N)$ for nontrivial~$\chi$---ruling out
  permanent avoidance of any residue class.
\end{enumerate}
These are related by proved implications
($\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Dec} \Rightarrow \mathrm{PED}$)
and direct bypass ($\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \mathrm{CCSB}$).
Each implies MC through the verified reduction chain.  The irreducible
mathematical content is: does the factoring operation destroy enough
correlation for character sums to cancel?


%% =========================================================================
\subsection{The NAAD Conjecture}
\label{sec:naad}
%% =========================================================================

The preceding analysis---the four-way blocker, the telescope exhaustion,
the dead-end catalogue---identifies a structural gap that no existing
theory covers.  We now name this gap and state a precise conjecture.

\paragraph{Three theories that almost apply.}
Three mature mathematical theories converge on the class of problems to
which MC belongs, without covering it.

\emph{Arithmetic dynamics} (Silverman, Baker--DeMarco) studies iteration
of algebraic maps $\varphi \colon \mathbb{P}^1 \to \mathbb{P}^1$ over
number fields.  The theory requires the map to be a \emph{fixed}
polynomial or rational function; the EM map
$T(P) = P \cdot \minFac(P{+}1)$ is non-algebraic, since the multiplier
depends on the factorization of~$P{+}1$.

\emph{Multiplicative number theory} (Alladi, Hildebrand, Granville,
Soundararajan) gives precise asymptotics for the distribution of
$\minFac$, $\max\mathrm{Fac}$, $\Omega$, $\omega$ \emph{over the
population} of integers.  But it does not control these functions along
a specific deterministic orbit.

\emph{Computational pseudorandomness} (Blum--Micali, Goldreich--Levin)
studies when deterministic iteration produces outputs
indistinguishable from random.  The theory requires computational
hardness (one-wayness against polynomial-time adversaries) and operates
in the Boolean setting $\{0,1\}^n$, not the arithmetic setting~$\ZZ$.

The EM sequence sits at the intersection: an iterated map on~$\ZZ$
(arithmetic dynamics) whose multiplier is~$\minFac$ (multiplicative
number theory), whose pseudorandom behavior---if true---arises from
the opacity of integer factorization (computational pseudorandomness).

\paragraph{Arithmetic iteration maps.}

\begin{definition}[\defname{Arithmetic iteration map}]
\label{def:aim}
An \emph{arithmetic iteration map} is a function
$T \colon \ZZ_{>0} \to \ZZ_{>0}$ of the form $T(x) = x \cdot m(x)$,
where $m(x) \in \ZZ_{>1}$ is the \emph{multiplier}, determined by the
arithmetic of~$x$.  The orbit from seed~$x_0$ is the sequence
$x_0, T(x_0), T^2(x_0), \ldots$
\end{definition}

\noindent The map is \emph{non-autonomous}: the multiplier $m(x_n)$
depends on the state~$x_n$, which changes at every step.  Unlike
autonomous dynamics (a fixed map applied repeatedly), the effective
transformation changes at each iteration.

\begin{example}[\defname{Examples}]
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Euclid--Mullin}: $T(P) = P \cdot \minFac(P{+}1)$.
\item \textbf{Largest-factor Euclid}: $T(P) = P \cdot \max\mathrm{Fac}(P{+}1)$.
  Same structure, different selection rule; provably misses
  primes~\cite{CoxVdP1968}.
\item \textbf{Random-factor Euclid}: $T(P) = P \cdot
  \mathrm{rand\-Fac}(P{+}1)$.  The walk is a genuine random walk on
  $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$; equidistribution follows from
  Diaconis--Shahshahani mixing.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\noindent For a prime~$q$, the \emph{walk} is the projection
$w_n = T^n(x_0) \bmod q$ onto $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$, satisfying the
multiplicative recurrence $w_{n+1} = w_n \cdot m_n \bmod q$ where
$m_n = m(x_n) \bmod q$.  This is the walk framework of
Section~\ref{sec:walk}, now abstracted to a class of maps.

\paragraph{Four structural properties.}
We identify four properties that control the walk's behavior.  All four
are verified for the EM map (the first three are proved; the fourth
follows from standard ANT).

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{CRT-blindness} (= Property~1 of \S\ref{sec:crt-equidist}):
  $m(x) \bmod q$ is asymptotically independent of $x \bmod q$ in the
  population.  For EM, this is the algebraic fact
  \lean{EM/Group/CRT.lean}{crt\_multiplier\_invariance}.

\item \textbf{Super-exponential growth}: $T^n(x_0) \geq 2^{2^n}$ for
  $n \geq n_0$.  For EM, $\Prod{2}(n) \geq 2^{n+1}$
  (\lean{EM/LargeSieve/Structural.lean}{prod\_lower\_bound\_for\_sieve}).
  This creates the information bottleneck: the walk position uses
  $O(\log q)$ bits to describe a state with $\Omega(2^n)$ bits.

\item \textbf{Generating multipliers}: the residues
  $\{m(x_n) \bmod q\}_{n \geq 0}$ generate $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$.
  For EM, this is SubgroupEscape, proved unconditionally via PRE
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:pre}).

\item \textbf{Population equidistribution}: $m(x) \bmod q$ is
  equidistributed over $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$ in the population.  For EM
  at a fixed modulus this is \emph{false} (head domination, Dead
  End~\#160, \S\ref{sec:ant-def}); it holds only in the double limit
  $z \to \infty$ over $z$-rough integers.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}[\defname{NAAD Equidistribution}]
\label{conj:naad}
Let $T(x) = x \cdot m(x)$ be an arithmetic iteration map satisfying
Properties~1--4 for all primes $q \geq q_0$.  Let $x_0 \in \ZZ_{>0}$
with $\gcd(x_0, q) = 1$.  Then the walk $w_n = T^n(x_0) \bmod q$
equidistributes in $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$:
\[
  \text{for every non-trivial } \chi \colon (\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times \to
  \mathbb{C}^\times,\quad
  \frac{1}{N}\Bigl|\sum_{n < N} \chi(w_n)\Bigr| \;\to\; 0
  \quad\text{as } N \to \infty.
\]
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}
NAAD Equidistribution for the EM map implies MC.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The EM map satisfies Properties~1--4.  NAAD applied with $x_0 = 2$
gives CCSB, which implies MC through the machine-verified chain
$\mathrm{CCSB} \Rightarrow \mathrm{MC}$
(\lean{EM/Equidist/SelfCorrecting.lean}{complex\_csb\_mc'}).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The three-level gap.}
NAAD Equidistribution is the assertion that Level~3 of the CRT
hierarchy (\S\ref{sec:crt-equidist}) follows from Levels~1--2.
The conjecture's content is precisely the orbit-specificity
transfer: Properties~1--4 describe the \emph{function}~$m$;
the conjecture asks whether the \emph{orbit} inherits the function's
statistical behavior.  The entire infrastructure of this
formalization---58{,}150~lines---reduces to this single question.

\paragraph{Relation to Collatz.}
Tao~\cite{Tao2019Collatz} proved that almost all Collatz orbits attain almost
bounded values, using a random model for Syracuse random variables.
Both Collatz and EM involve non-autonomous iteration maps with CRT
structure; both have random models that behave well; both face the
transfer problem from random model to actual orbit.  The key
difference: the Collatz map is piecewise linear mod~2 (clean
algebraic structure in the 2-adic coordinate), while the EM map
involves factoring, which has no algebraic shortcut.  This makes EM
harder to analyze but also more ``pseudorandom''---the factoring
channel is a stronger scrambler than the 2-adic valuation.

\paragraph{Why NAAD requires new mathematics.}
The four-way blocker (\S\ref{sec:hash-heuristic}) applies to any
arithmetic iteration map: Properties~1--4 explicitly exclude
independence, multiplicativity, algebraic-geometric structure, and
ergodic stationarity.  NAAD Equidistribution asks whether these four
properties \emph{collectively} substitute for any single classical
assumption.  The \DEentries~dead ends documented in the formalization
(Appendix~\ref{app:dead-ends}) confirm that no existing technique
closes this gap.

\paragraph{NAAD open problems.}
Beyond the full conjecture, several intermediate problems are
independently interesting:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Weak NAAD} (almost-all starting points): prove that for
  almost all squarefree~$x_0$, the walk equidistributes.  Partially
  achieved: SD~$\Rightarrow$~cancellation for a.a.\ starting points
  (\lean{EM/Ensemble/PT.lean}{sd\_implies\_cancellation}).

\item \textbf{Quantitative NAAD} (correlation decay): prove
  $|\mathrm{Cov}(\chi(w_n), \chi(w_{n+k}))| \leq C \alpha^{-k}$
  for some $\alpha > 1$.  This would give NAAD via van der Corput
  with $|\sum \chi(w_n)| = O(\sqrt{N})$.

\item \textbf{CRTPropagationStep}: prove that accumulator
  equidistribution propagates across ensemble steps.  This is a
  population-level statement with no orbit-specificity barrier,
  amenable to CRT~$+$~Alladi.

\item \textbf{NAAD for other maps}: study variants
  $T(x) = x \cdot \max\mathrm{Fac}(x{+}1)$,
  $T(x) = x \cdot \Omega(x{+}1)$, etc.  The comparison illuminates the
  role of the selection rule: minFac gives slow growth and guaranteed
  capture (\S\ref{sec:selectability}); maxFac gives fast growth and
  provable failure.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{The Precise Frontier}
\label{sec:precise-frontier}

The formalization certifies the reductions above and pinpoints the
frontier.  We now identify the open problems whose resolution
would close Mullin's Conjecture.

\paragraph{CCSB as the Precise Frontier}

The formalization identifies \textbf{ComplexCharSumBound} as the
irreducible analytic content.  The question:

\begin{center}
\emph{Are the walk character sums $\sum_{n<N} \chi(\walkZ{2}{q}{n})$ bounded
$o(N)$ for every non-trivial $\chi$?}
\end{center}

CCSB is a single hypothesis that implies MC with no additional
conditions.  As shown in Section~\ref{sec:character}, CCSB rules out
permanent avoidance of~$-1$: the Fourier bridge forces the hit count
at~$-1$ to be $N/(q{-}1) + o(N)$, contradicting the zero hits that
the first missing prime's walk must achieve past the sieve gap.

The walk telescoping identities (Section~\ref{sec:character}) provide
precise structural constraints.  The identity
$\sum_n \chi(w(n))(\chi(m(n))-1) = O(1)$ means that the walk sum
$S_N$ and the multiplier sum $M_N = \sum_n \chi(m(n))$ satisfy
$S_N \approx S_N + (M_N - S_N) = M_N + O(1)$ only in the crude sense;
the telescoping does \emph{not} separate them.

\paragraph{Connection to Bombieri--Vinogradov}

A Bombieri--Vinogradov type result for EM walk residues would give:
\[
\sum_{\substack{q \leq Q \\ q\text{ prime}}}
\max_{a}
\Bigl| |\{n \leq N : w(n) \equiv a \pmod{q}\}|
  - \frac{N}{q-1} \Bigr|
\;\ll\; \frac{NQ}{(\log N)^A}.
\]
For non-exceptional primes, the hit count at~$-1$ would be
$\sim N/(q{-}1) > 0$, contradicting permanent avoidance; the finitely
many exceptional primes below~$Q_0$ are handled by the inductive
bootstrap (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}), closing the conjecture.

The difficulty is that BV applies to the set of \emph{all} primes,
not to a specific subsequence.  The EM walk is deterministic and
self-referential: the walk at step~$n$ depends on the factorization
of~$\Prod{2}(n)+1$, which depends on all previous walk values.  Standard
BV does not apply.

\paragraph{Connection to Chebotarev}

The \textbf{EffectiveKummerEscape} hypothesis asserts: for each
prime~$\ell$, there exists~$B$ such that for $q \geq B$ with
$\ell \mid q\!-\!1$, some multiplier among the first~$B$ escapes the
$\ell$-th power kernel.  This is a Chebotarev-type statement for the
Kummer extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell, 3^{1/\ell}, \ldots,
53^{1/\ell})$: the Frobenius at~$q$ determines which multiplier
primes are $\ell$-th power residues.

An effective Chebotarev density theorem~\cite{LagariasOdlyzko1977}
for this fixed number field
would give EKE for all but finitely many~$q$ (effectively bounded).
Combined with finite verification for the remaining~$q$, this would
prove PRE and hence SE unconditionally---but SE is
\emph{already} proved unconditionally via the elementary PRE.
The Chebotarev approach would give a stronger \emph{effective}
bound on how quickly SE kicks in, refining the density argument of
\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}.

\paragraph{The Sieve-Theoretic Approach}

\textbf{Mertens\-Escape}: for any prime~$q$ and proper
subgroup~$H$, infinitely many primes outside~$H$ exist
(Dirichlet content).
\textbf{Sieve\-Amplification}: Mertens escape should force
eventual $\minFac(\Prod{2}(n){+}1)$ escape from~$H$, via
super-exponential growth and mutual coprimality of
successive Euclid numbers.

The formally verified chain:
$\mathrm{MertensEscape} + \mathrm{SieveAmplification}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{TailSE} \xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{CofinalEscape} \xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{QuotientDH}$.

The formalization articulates a richer sieve infrastructure in two parallel
routes:

\paragraph{Cumulative route.}
\begin{gather*}
\mathrm{PDE}
\xrightarrow{\text{Alladi}}
\mathrm{GLPFE}
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SieveTransfer}}
\mathrm{SieveEquidist}
\xrightarrow{\text{open}}
\mathrm{NoLongRuns} \\
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{PED}
\xrightarrow{\text{open}}
\mathrm{CCSB}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{MC}.
\end{gather*}
Here PDE is PrimeDensityEquipartition (PNT in arithmetic progressions,
a known theorem not yet in Mathlib), and GLPFE is GenericLPFEquidist
(Alladi's theorem~\cite{Alladi1977} on $\minFac$ distribution of generic integers,
also known but not formalized).  Both ends of the chain---from PDE to
GLPFE via Alladi, and from CCSB to MC via Fourier inversion---are
formally proved.

\paragraph{Window route.}
\[
\mathrm{StrongSieveEquidist}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{NoLongRunsAt}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{PEDAt}
\xrightarrow{\text{open}}
\mathrm{CCSB}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{MC}.
\]
StrongSieveEquidist asserts that EM multipliers are equidistributed
within sliding windows; NoLongRunsAt and PEDAt are per-prime variants
proved by pigeonhole and block-counting respectively
(\lean{EM/Equidist/SieveTransfer.lean}{strongSieveEquidist\_noLongRunsAt},
\lean{EM/Equidist/SieveTransfer.lean}{noLongRunsAt\_ped}).

\paragraph{The genuine frontier.}

An earlier draft placed it at \textbf{SieveTransfer}: ``does the
equidistribution of $\minFac$ residues for generic integers transfer
to the specific EM orbit?''  There is no such generic
equidistribution to transfer: for $q$-rough integers the class
densities of $\minFac \bmod q$ are head-dominated convergent series
(Dead End~\#160), and SieveTransfer holds vacuously.  The frontier is
the orbit statement itself---StrongSieveEquidist / CME: the EM Euclid
numbers are determined by the entire walk history, and no population
fact supplies the residue of their least prime factor.

\paragraph{Sieve-to-harmonic convergence.}

The sieve hierarchy (\S36--\S39 of \code{EM/Equidist/SelfCorrecting.lean})
and the harmonic hierarchy (\S30--\S35) converge: both produce
DecorrelationHypothesis as output.  The full chain
\[
\mathrm{SieveEquidist}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{Dec}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{PED}
\xrightarrow[\text{sole gap}]{\text{open}}
\mathrm{CCSB}
\xrightarrow{\text{proved}}
\mathrm{MC}
\]
is formalized, with the first two arrows machine-verified
(\lean{EM/LargeSieve/Structural.lean}{sieve\_equidist\_implies\_decorrelation},
\lean{EM/Equidist/SelfCorrecting.lean}{decorrelation\_implies\_ped}).
The sieve route achieves SieveEquidist~$\Rightarrow$~Dec via a
counting-to-character-sum bridge: SieveEquidistribution produces
\code{EMMultCharSumBound} with $Q_0 = 0$, meaning multiplier character
sums cancel for \emph{all} primes~$q$, which is exactly
DecorrelationHypothesis.  The sole remaining gap on this route is
\textbf{PEDImpliesComplexCSB}
(\lean{EM/Equidist/SelfCorrecting.lean}{PEDImpliesComplexCSB}):
does positive escape density for all primes imply walk character sum
cancellation?  Any proof of SieveEquidistribution (e.g., from PNT
in APs $+$ Alladi's theorem) would immediately yield Dec and PED
for free, isolating this single bridge as the only open step.

\paragraph{CME: The Sole Remaining Hypothesis}

The reduction landscape (Section~\ref{sec:ensemble}) collapses to a
single open statement: \textbf{Conditional Multiplier
Equidistribution}~(CME, Property~\ref{def:cme}).  The assembly
$\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \mathrm{CCSB} \Rightarrow \mathrm{MC}$ is
fully machine-verified
(\lean{EM/CME/Reduction.lean}{cme\_implies\_mc}).  An earlier draft
placed a further hypothesis beneath it, a transfer lemma from
population-level equidistribution to the orbit; the population
statement is false (Dead End~\#160, \S\ref{sec:ant-def}), so CME is
where the orbit-specificity barrier now sits, undisguised.

\paragraph{The Adelic Decomposition of CME}

The adelic framework (Section~\ref{sec:character}) decomposes CME into
two independent components:

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Adelic/Equidist.lean}{cme\_iff\_adelic}]
$\mathrm{CME} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \mathrm{MWI} \,\wedge\, \mathrm{MME}$,
\end{theorem}

\noindent where \textbf{MWI} (Multiplier--Walk Independence) asserts
that the conditional distribution of the multiplier given the walk
position converges to the marginal, and \textbf{MME} (Marginal Multiplier
Equidistribution) asserts that the marginal multiplier distribution
converges to uniform.

The decomposition is proved in both directions
(\code{cme\_implies\_mme}, \code{crt\_fiber\_mme\_implies\_cme}).
MWI is the core difficulty: it is the formal statement that the
factoring channel destroys the correlation between walk position and
multiplier residue.  MME is the orbit-level equidistribution of the multiplier
residues themselves---a statement about the specific orbit that no
population result supplies (Dead End~\#160).

\noindent The CRT fiber structure provides a further decomposition:
\[
\mathrm{CRTFiber} + \mathrm{MME}
\;\xRightarrow{\text{proved}}\;
\mathrm{MWI}
\;\xRightarrow[\text{+ MME}]{\text{proved}}\;
\mathrm{CME}
\;\xRightarrow{\text{proved}}\;
\mathrm{MC}.
\]
CRTFiber (the CRT fiber determines the next multiplier, proved in
\code{crt\_fiber\_determines\_genSeq}) provides the structural basis for MWI\@.
The proof uses Fourier inversion on the character indicator function
(\code{crt\_fiber\_implies\_mwi\_proved}, $\sim$145 lines).

MME is equivalent to vanishing walk lag-1 autocorrelation
(\code{mme\_iff\_walk\_autocorrelation}), providing a purely
walk-theoretic reformulation.  The landscape theorem
\code{ccsb\_mme\_landscape} proves: CME~$\Rightarrow$~CCSB~$\wedge$~MME,
and conversely CCSB~$\wedge$~MME~$+$~MWI~$\Rightarrow$~CME\@.

\paragraph{The Profinite Weyl Criterion}

The \textbf{Uniform Profinite Equidistribution} (UPE) hypothesis
asserts: for every $k \geq 1$ and every $k$-tuple of distinct primes
$(q_1, \ldots, q_k)$, the joint distribution of
$(w_{q_1}(n), \ldots, w_{q_k}(n))$ converges to uniform on
$\prod_i (\ZZ/q_i\ZZ)^\times$.  UPE is the profinite analog of
equidistribution: it asks for uniform distribution modulo all finite
sets of primes simultaneously.

\begin{theorem}[\lean{EM/Adelic/Profinite.lean}{uniform\_profinite\_implies\_mc}]
$\mathrm{UPE} \;\Longrightarrow\; \MC$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent The proof: UPE at $k = 1$ gives CCSB
(\code{uniform\_profinite\_implies\_ccsb}), which suffices.
UPE at $k = 2$ gives CPD (Conditional Pairwise Decorrelation,
\code{uniform\_profinite\_implies\_cpd}).  Dead End~\#125 shows
that pairwise ($k = 2$) does NOT imply $k$-wise---the XOR
counterexample is decisive---so UPE is genuinely stronger than
CCSB~$\wedge$~CPD\@.

The routes to MC via the adelic/profinite framework are collected in
\code{profinite\_routes\_to\_mc} (3-route conjunction: UPE, CCSB,
adelic, all proved).

\paragraph{The profinite walk.}
The EM accumulator maps to
$\hat{\ZZ}^\times = \varprojlim (\ZZ/N\ZZ)^\times$ via its residues.
If a prime~$q$ is missed, the walk stays in
$(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times \setminus \{-1\}$ permanently.
For an infinite set~$M$ of missed primes, the walk inhabits the
\emph{survival set}
$\mathcal{S}_M = \prod_{q \in M}\bigl((\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times \setminus \{-1\}\bigr)$,
which has Haar measure $\prod_{q \in M}(q{-}2)/(q{-}1) = 0$.
The EM walk thus permanently occupies a measure-zero subset of the
profinite group---consistent for a deterministic sequence, but
constraining.

The walk in a product
$(\ZZ/q_1\ZZ)^\times \times (\ZZ/q_2\ZZ)^\times$ is
\emph{synchronously coupled}: both coordinates are multiplied by the
same factor~$f(n)$ at each step.  The orbit is a coset of the diagonal
multiplier subgroup
$\Delta = \{(f,f) : f \in \langle\text{multipliers}\rangle\}$.
PrimeResidueEscape gives generation in each factor separately; the
joint question---whether the multipliers generate the full product
$(\ZZ/q_1 q_2\ZZ)^\times$---is a CRT lifting problem not implied by
individual generation.  Dense orbit in~$\hat{\ZZ}^\times$ would give
MC directly.

\paragraph{What Would Close the Conjecture}

\begin{frontierbox}[title={\sffamily\bfseries Paths to MC}]
The cleanest paths to MC:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prove CME} (the reformulation of MC in walk language): show that the
  multiplier character sum $\sum_{\substack{n < N \\ w(n) = c}} \chi(m(n))$ is
  $o(N)$ for each walk position~$c$, for every missing~$q$.  CME implies
  CCSB (\lean{EM/CME/Reduction.lean}{cme\_implies\_ccsb}) and is equivalent
  to MC (\lean{EM/CME/Equivalences.lean}{cme\_iff\_mc}).
  CME asks only about the \emph{conditional} distribution of
  multipliers given walk state---it does not require controlling the
  walk character sum itself.  This bypasses the $d \geq 3$ barrier
  entirely.

\item \textbf{Prove CCSB directly}: show that no non-trivial
  character sum can sustain the $\Omega(N)$ bias that permanent
  avoidance of~$-1$ would require.  The self-correcting sieve
  (concentration of EM primes in a residue class is exponentially
  self-limiting) is the strongest heuristic argument.

\item \textbf{Exhibit one horizon} (the weakest form of the previous
  item).  Fix the least missing prime~$q$ and find a \emph{single}
  window past the sieve gap with
  \[
    \sum_{\chi \neq 1}
      \Bigl\lvert \sum_{n < N} \chi(\walkZ{2}{q}{n}) \Bigr\rvert < N .
  \]
  Fourier inversion then forces the walk to visit \emph{every} unit
  before~$N$, in particular~$-1$
  (\lean{EM/Equidist/OneHorizon.lean}{covers\_of\_charSum\_lt}), and one
  such window at each prime gives MC
  (\lean{EM/Equidist/OneHorizon.lean}{windowFourierGain\_implies\_mc}).
  This asks for one finite-horizon gain rather than $o(N)$ cancellation
  at every large~$N$.

\item \textbf{Prove a BV-type estimate}: even an averaged bound
  over~$q$ would suffice---for $q \geq Q_0$ it rules out permanent
  avoidance, and the finitely many $q < Q_0$ are handled by the
  inductive bootstrap (\S\ref{sec:bootstrap}).

\item \textbf{Close the orbit chain gap}: show that at each cofinally
  visited walk position, at least two distinct multiplier classes
  appear.  This would force the orbit chain to expand to the full group.

\item \textbf{Prove DH directly}: show that a multiplicative walk
  on a cyclic group with a generating set of multipliers must hit
  every element cofinally.  This is a combinatorial question about
  deterministic walks.

\item \textbf{Prove StrongSieveEquidist}: show that the EM orbit's
  $\minFac$ residues equidistribute within sliding windows; the window
  route (\S38) then gives PED and, with the CCSB bridge, MC\@.  (The
  former ``SieveTransfer'' item is retired: its generic-integer input is
  false, Dead End~\#160.)

\item \textbf{Prove BVImpliesMMCSB or PrimeArithLSImpliesMMCSB}: the
  large sieve route (\S41--\S65) reduces MC to a transfer hypothesis.
  Given Bombieri--Vinogradov or the analytic large sieve, the remaining
  open step is showing that the EM orbit's multipliers receive their
  fair share of each residue class---in particular, that the death
  channel is not systematically avoided.  This is the same
  orbit-specificity gap as CME, approached from a different
  mathematical toolkit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{frontierbox}

\paragraph{A caveat on the one-horizon route.}
It is weaker as a \emph{global} statement---one window rather than every
large one---but \emph{stronger per character} at that window.  With
$q-2$ nontrivial characters the hypothesis needs an average
$\lvert S_\chi \rvert < N/(q-2)$, a saving by a factor of order~$q$,
not merely ``some cancellation''.  For a walk with square-root
cancellation it bites once $N \gtrsim q^2$, so the honest reading is a
quantitative target: \emph{past the sieve gap, the walk covers
$(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$ within $O(q^2)$ steps}.  That is a better-posed open
problem than $o(N)$---finite, quantitative, partial progress
measurable---but it is not an easier one.  No technique bounds walk
character sums for the actual orbit at \emph{any} horizon; the entire
difficulty lies between the trivial bound~$N$ and anything below it, and
that gap is indifferent to the quantifier over~$N$.

\paragraph{The one unconditional constraint at a missing prime.}
Every item above is a hypothesis.  There is exactly one hard fact the
least missing prime hands over for free, and it is combinatorial rather
than distributional.  Past the stage at which all primes below~$q$ have
appeared, injectivity forbids any of them from being selected again and
$q$~itself is excluded, so \emph{every subsequent multiplier is a prime
strictly greater than~$q$}
(\lean{EM/Equidist/OneHorizon.lean}{multipliers\_exceed}); equivalently
$\Prod{2}(n)+1$ is $q$-rough for all large~$n$
(\lean{EM/Equidist/OneHorizon.lean}{rough\_at\_missing}).

This survives whatever the character sums do.  It is also, so far,
unexploitable: distinctness is a statement about the primes, while the
walk sees only residues mod~$q$, and the projection
$p \mapsto p \bmod q$ destroys exactly the information the constraint
carries---Dead End~\#152 in a specific instance.  What it does say is
that a missing prime confines the orbit forever to the $q$-rough set, of
density $\prod_{p<q}(1-1/p) \approx e^{-\gamma}/\log q$: small, but
positive, and nothing forbids a single trajectory from remaining in a
positive-density set forever.  That sentence is the whole difficulty in
miniature.

\paragraph{The vanishing noise route.}
A complementary perspective recasts the walk as a product of random
and deterministic steps.  At each step, $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ has a set~$F$
of prime factors; $\minFac$ selects the smallest, but the
\emph{factor set} $F \bmod q$ acts as a ``noisy generating set'' in
$(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$.  The spectral gap lemma
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{spectral\_gap\_with\_identity})
proves: for any generating set $S \ni 1$ in a finite abelian group
and any nontrivial character~$\chi$,
$\|\sum_{s \in S} \chi(s)\| < |S|$
(strict inequality, using strict convexity of~$\mathbb{C}$).
Deterministic walk steps---multiplying by a fixed element---preserve
this contraction
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{char\_sum\_mul\_right\_norm}).
If the factor set has $|F| \geq 2$ elements in $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$
infinitely often (with non-summable spectral gap), the product
$\prod(1 - \gamma_k) \to 0$
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{product\_contraction\_tendsto}),
which would give CCSB---but only if the walk actually \emph{averages}
over~$F$.  A proved counterexample
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{selection\_vs\_average\_gap})
shows the gap cleanly: for any fixed selection $s \in S$, the
pointwise norm $\|\chi(s)\| = 1$ (zero contraction), even when the
average over~$S$ contracts strictly below~$1$.
Since $\minFac$ selects \emph{one specific element} of the factor set,
the spectral gap alone does not imply CCSB\@.

The formalization defines the factor set residues
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{factorSetResidues})
and proves that the walk multiplier lies in it
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoise.lean}{multZ\_in\_factorSetResidues}).
Two questions remain, recorded in Lean only as placeholder
propositions (\code{InfinitelyManyLargeFactorSets},
\code{MinFacUnbiased}; bodies \code{True}):
that the Euclid numbers $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ have at least two prime factors
$\geq q$ for infinitely many~$n$, and that $\minFac$ selection is
asymptotically unbiased over the squarefree ensemble.
The latter is the orbit-specificity barrier
restated in spectral language.

The formalization makes the gap precise via a \emph{phase transition}
at~$\varepsilon = 0$
(\lean{EM/Stochastic/VanishingNoiseC.lean}{phase\_transition\_landscape}).
For any constant~$\varepsilon \in (0,1)$, the two-point character
product $\prod_k[(1{-}\varepsilon)\chi(a_k) + \varepsilon\chi(b_k)]$
decays to~$0$ in norm whenever infinitely many steps have distinct
$\chi$-values---a uniform spectral gap (finite-group pigeonhole)
ensures non-summable contraction.  At~$\varepsilon = 0$, each factor
reduces to~$\chi(a_k)$ with $|\chi(a_k)| = 1$, so the product norm is
identically~$1$.  MC~(via CCSB) is exactly the assertion that the
\emph{Cesàro average} of these unit-modulus products cancels.
The transition is sharp: any~$\varepsilon > 0$ gives decay; $\varepsilon = 0$
gives perpetual oscillation on the unit circle.

The vanishing noise perspective also motivates the
$\{\minFac, \operatorname{secondMinFac}\}$ variant MC
(Section~\ref{sec:minfac-secondminfac}), which proves that
\emph{some} selection from two-point factor sets reaches every
residue class---under a single open spectral hypothesis
(UFDStrong); for $q = 3$, a faithful character escape argument
closes UFDStrong unconditionally (Section~\ref{sec:variants}).
The stochastic MC chain, the self-consistent branching tree,
and the weak Mullin weakenings are developed in
Section~\ref{sec:variants}; the function field analog
(Section~\ref{sec:function-field}) confirms that the difficulty is
orbit-specificity, not analytic number theory.


\paragraph{Function Field Analog}

Over $\FF_p[t]$, the FF-EM sequence has additional structural
advantages.  The formalization proves
(\code{EM/FunctionField/WeakMC.lean}, 471~lines, zero \code{sorry}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Degree escape}
(\lean{EM/FunctionField/WeakMC.lean}{ffSeq\_degree\_tendsto\_atTop}):
$\deg(\mathrm{ffSeq}(n)) \to \infty$, unconditional from finiteness
$+$ injectivity.
\item \textbf{Capture count stabilization}: for each degree~$d$, the
number of degree-$d$ irreducibles captured eventually stabilizes.
\item \textbf{Pool partition identity}
(\lean{EM/FunctionField/WeakMC.lean}{captureCount\_plus\_missing}):
captured $+$ unsieved $=$ total, an exact accounting.
\item \textbf{FFCaptureExhaustive $\Leftrightarrow$ FFMullinConjecture}:
the two natural formulations are equivalent.
\end{itemize}
The pool partition identity gives quantitative control:
if the total number of monic irreducibles of degree~$\leq D$ is~$M$,
then among the first~$N > M$ terms, at least one has degree~$> D$.
Dead End~\#129 (FFLM structurally false---cyclotomic Galois groups)
is sidestepped entirely: degree escape uses only finiteness and
injectivity, not monodromy.

\subsection{Does the Walk Hit $-1$?}
\label{sec:does-it-hit}
%% =========================================================================

The irreducible open question is not whether DH holds (cofinal
hitting is a convenient sufficient condition) but whether the walk
hits~$-1$ \emph{at least once} past the sieve gap.  The Single Hit
Theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:single-hit}) shows that a single hit at
each prime suffices for MC; DH, CCSB, and CME are strategies for
producing that hit.

\paragraph{Evidence for.}
Three independent lines of evidence suggest the walk does hit~$-1$
(see also Remark~\ref{rem:why-dsl}).
(1)~\emph{The information bottleneck}: $\Prod{2}(n)$ has
${\sim}2^n$ bits, but the walk position carries only $O(\log q)$ bits.
The smallest prime factor of $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ depends on the full
value, not on its residue mod~$q$, so conditioning on walk position
constrains almost nothing about the factoring outcome---the
conditional and unconditional $\minFac$ distributions should agree.
(2)~\emph{Ensemble structure}: earlier drafts cited here a proved
``SD~$\Rightarrow$~cancellation'' chain as evidence that CME holds for
almost all starting points; SD is false as stated (Dead End~\#156), so
that evidence is withdrawn.  What remains is structural: the multiplier
is CRT-blind to the walk position, and $2$ is one squarefree seed among
many---a typicality \emph{heuristic}.
(3)~\emph{Analogy with Artin's conjecture}: Artin's conjecture
(that every non-square integer is a primitive root for infinitely
many primes) has the same orbit-specificity structure and is believed
true; Hooley~\cite{Hooley1967} proved it conditional on GRH.  The
walk-hitting question is the analogous statement for the EM walk
and would follow from an analogous uniformity hypothesis.

\paragraph{Evidence against.}
Three features of the EM sequence give pause.
(0)~\emph{The autonomous branch}: there is an explicit, unconditional
mechanism by which MC fails.  If $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ is prime for all
large~$n$, then $\minFac(\Prod{2}(n)+1) = \Prod{2}(n)+1$, the accumulator
update closes up into the autonomous quadratic map
$w \mapsto w^2 + w$ on $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$, and MC is \emph{false}---by
Bertrand, some prime in $(\Prod{2}(T)+1,\, 2(\Prod{2}(T)+1)]$ is skipped
forever
(\lean{EM/Population/AutonomousBranch.lean}{eventually\_prime\_implies\_not\_mullin};
no congruence condition and no witness are needed).  On that branch the
death equation $w^2+w+1 = 0$ has no solution in $\FF_q$ whenever
$q \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, so the walk is not merely unlucky---it is
\emph{structurally} barred from the death class, on a density-$1/2$ set
of primes.

This is the sharpest answer to the natural objection that an erratic
walk on a finite cyclic group must be killed sooner or later.  The
erraticness is not a permanent feature of the dynamics: it is a
consequence of the Euclid numbers being \emph{composite}, which is what
supplies a fresh, unconstrained multiplier at each step.  Perpetual
primality would end the erraticness outright.  Every heuristic for MC
therefore silently assumes (C$\infty$)---that infinitely many
$\Prod{2}(n)+1$ are composite (Dead End~\#146)---and (C$\infty$) is
itself open.
\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{The branch is not exotic---the sequence has been on it.}  Both
$\Prod{2}(6)+1 = 6221671$ and $\Prod{2}(7)+1 = 38709183810571$ are prime,
so at stages~$6$ and~$7$ the least factor \emph{was} the whole Euclid
number and the accumulator took the autonomous step
$P \mapsto P(P+1)$ twice in a row
(\lean{EM/Population/SylvesterTower.lean}{euclid\_prime\_at\_six},
\lean{EM/Population/SylvesterTower.lean}{autonomous\_step\_at\_six}).
The branch is a regime the sequence visits, not a fantasy.  It broke out
at the next stage:
\[
  \Prod{2}(8)+1 = 1498400911280533294827535471 = 139 \cdot 10779862671083836653435507 .
\]

\begin{theorem}[The branch cannot begin at or before stage~$7$ ---
\lean{EM/Population/SylvesterTower.lean}{not\_perpetualPrimality\_of\_le\_seven}]
\label{thm:no-perpetual-le-seven}
For every $N \leq 7$, perpetual primality from~$N$ is false.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
The proof needs no primality certificate for the fourteen-digit
$\Prod{2}(7)+1$, because the hypothesis supplies what would otherwise
have to be proved: perpetual primality from $N \leq 7$ gives primality at
stage~$7$, hence forces $\Prod{2}(8) = \Prod{2}(7)\bigl(\Prod{2}(7)+1\bigr)$,
and then demands that $\Prod{2}(8)+1$ be prime---which the factor~$139$
refutes.

\smallskip\noindent
\textbf{The branch to be excluded is exactly this one---nothing wider.}
Everything above attacks the extreme point $\seq{2}(n+1) = \Prod{2}(n)+1$,
where the dynamics degenerates to an autonomous map and can therefore be
analysed; it is fair to ask whether the true failure set is larger, with
the analysable branch a corner of it.  It is not:
\S\ref{sec:defect-telescope} shows the growth constant
$C = \lim_N \log\Prod{2}(N)/2^N$ is a \emph{complete invariant}, with
$C = 0 \iff (\mathrm{C}\infty)$ and $C > 0 \iff$ perpetual primality from
some stage.  So the failure set of $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$ is precisely the
autonomous branch, and every mechanism in this section bears on the whole
of it.

\paragraph{How the sequence escapes, quantified.}
The escape mechanism is sharper than ``large numbers are rarely prime''.
On the branch $q_{n+1} = \Phi_6(q_n) = q_n^2 - q_n + 1$, so a prime
$\ell$ divides $q_{n+1}$ exactly when $q_n$ is a root of $\Phi_6$
modulo~$\ell$---which requires $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 6$.  The map $\Phi_6$
has the unique fixed point~$1$, with $0 \mapsto 1$, and the only
preimages of~$1$ are $0$ and~$1$; so an orbit not starting at~$1$ either
passes through~$0$---meaning $\ell$ divides some tower term and the
branch dies---or is trapped in some other cycle forever.

Write $\rho(\ell)$ for the proportion of residues whose $\Phi_6$-orbit
reaches~$0$.  Over the $207$ primes $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ below $3000$
the mean of $\rho$ is $\approx 0.072$, with $\rho(7) = 0.857$ and
$\rho(13) = 0.615$; the partial sums of $\rho$ grow without apparent
bound.  Perpetual primality therefore requires the tower to dodge
\emph{every} prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 6$ outside the bag,
simultaneously and forever, an event of heuristic probability
$\prod_\ell (1-\rho(\ell)) = 0$.  Note also that $\rho$ is defined
purely by the autonomous map, so unlike the rest of this paper the
heuristic here involves no non-autonomous dynamics at all.

\smallskip\noindent
That this does not become a proof is instructive.  It would suffice to
find many primes~$\ell$ for which \emph{every} orbit reaches~$0$, since
each such $\ell$ would then have to lie in the finite bag at the
branching stage; but a search over all $330$ primes $\ell \equiv 1
\pmod 6$ below~$5000$ finds exactly one, namely $\ell = 7$.  The
condition ``$\{1\}$ is the only cycle'' is rare, so the counting argument
is unavailable and one is left needing to control the specific orbit of
$\Prod{2}(N)+1$ modulo infinitely many primes.  That is the
orbit-specificity barrier again, in the one setting where the dynamics is
autonomous.

(1)~\emph{Cox--van der Poorten}~\cite{CoxVdP1968}: the ``largest factor'' variant of
the Euclid--Mullin sequence provably misses primes.  The EM
sequence's completeness is not a soft consequence of the Euclid
construction but depends sensitively on the $\minFac$ selection
rule.  This fragility means heuristic arguments
(``it should work because Euclid numbers have many factors'') are
not reliable.
(2)~\emph{The $d \geq 3$ barrier}: the formalization proves that
the most natural route from multiplier escape density to walk
character sum cancellation (PED~$\Rightarrow$~BRE~$\Rightarrow$~CCSB) is
\emph{impossible} for character orders $d \geq 3$.  This is not
evidence against the hit, but it shows that producing one
requires mechanisms beyond the simplest equidistribution
framework.  The CME bypass sidesteps this barrier, but the barrier's
existence means any proof must be genuinely subtle.

\paragraph{Assessment.}
We believe the walk does hit~$-1$ at every prime, primarily because
the self-correcting sieve mechanism provides a concrete dynamical
reason (not merely a probabilistic heuristic) for the walk to
eventually hit~$-1$.  The strongest form of this belief: CME should
hold because the EM multipliers, conditioned on walk position, have
no arithmetic reason to avoid the death channel systematically.
But we acknowledge that no existing technique can prove this.
The formalization has exhaustively explored all known proof strategies
across \DEentries~dead ends: the $d \geq 3$ barrier, the MultCancelToWalkCancel
counterexample (Dead End~\#117), the cofactor identity's failure to
escape the Marginal/Joint Barrier (Dead End~\#115), and the exhaustion
of all four CME proof directions (algebraic identities, cofactor
completeness, exponential sums, sum-product theory) collectively show
that the proof, when found, will require genuinely new mathematical
techniques---likely exploiting the specific arithmetic of the
$\minFac$ rule on super-exponentially growing squarefree numbers in
ways that current analytic number theory does not.

One direction remains genuinely untried: exploiting the
\emph{arithmetic of the seed} $x_0 = 2$.
The multiplicative order $\mathrm{ord}_q(2)$ is large for most
primes~$q$ (Artin's conjecture, proved under GRH by Hooley), and
character sums $\sum \chi(g^n)$ cancel when $\mathrm{ord}(g)$ is
large (Konyagin--Shparlinski).  This is exactly the pattern CCSB
demands.  The fatal gap: the walk position
$\Prod{2}(n) \bmod q = 2 \cdot \seq{2}(1) \cdots \seq{2}(n) \bmod q$
is \emph{not} the geometric progression $2^n \bmod q$---the
varying multipliers $\seq{2}(k)$ destroy the structure that makes
exponential-sum bounds work (Dead End~\#135).  No mechanism has been
found to convert Artin-type information about $\mathrm{ord}_q(2)$
into walk equidistribution through a route that avoids Fourier
analysis and Cauchy--Schwarz (Dead End~\#136).  Whether such a
mechanism exists is, in our view, the most promising open question.

\subsection{Four Questions}
\label{sec:four-questions}
%% =========================================================================

It is worth stating plainly what we take the formalization to say about
belief in the conjecture, since the negative results of this paper bear
on the question in a way that is easy to misread.

\paragraph{What if MC is false?}
Everything proved here is evidence against falsity, and
\emph{one-directionally}.  Every no-go theorem in
\S\ref{sec:min-max-dichotomy} rules out a proof that some prime is
\emph{omitted}: congruence invariants at every modulus, reciprocity
invariants, guarded fragments, anatomy carried as state.  None of them
rules out a proof that MC is \emph{true}.  The programme has been
accumulating a catalogue of ways one cannot show a prime is missed.

Moreover there is exactly one concrete mechanism by which MC could fail:
the autonomous branch.  It is consistent---nothing rules it out---but by
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-perpetual-le-seven} it cannot begin before stage~$8$,
and the escape estimate above puts its heuristic probability at zero by a
mechanism internal to the autonomous map.  The alternative failure mode,
``some~$q$ is simply never hit'', has no known mechanism at all, and a
\emph{proof} of it would have to be an omission proof---one of the shapes
this paper eliminates.  So if MC is false, we are unlikely ever to know.

The honest caution is that Euclid-type sequences genuinely can miss
primes: the $\mathrm{maxFac}$ sequence provably does.  What makes
$\minFac$ different is quantitative---slowest growth, most trials per
prime---not qualitative.

\paragraph{If MC is provable, what follows?}
As a statement MC is close to terminal; nothing famous hangs on it.  But
it has genuine open consequences, and this paper sharpens the strongest.
$\MC \Rightarrow (\mathrm{C}\infty)$, which
\S\ref{sec:sylvester-tower} identifies with a Sylvester-tower primality
question that is itself open; likewise
$\MC \Rightarrow \mathrm{WM} \Rightarrow \mathrm{RD}$ and
$\MC \Rightarrow (\mathrm{V})$, all open.  MC also says something clean
in its own right: the recursion $\seq{2}$ is a \emph{bijection}
$\NN \to \{\text{primes}\}$---a two-line recursion enumerating every
prime exactly once.

The more important point is that the machinery would be worth more than
the theorem.  Any proof must control $\Prod{2}(n) \bmod q$ for one
specific orbit.  Current technology---sieve, large sieve, Fourier,
Dirichlet---works over families and averages.  A proof of MC would be a
breakthrough in \emph{single-orbit} equidistribution, and that capability
would apply well beyond this sequence.

\paragraph{Does the Riemann Hypothesis imply MC?}
No such implication is known, and this development supplies a structural
reason to doubt one.  RH and GRH are population statements; MC needs the
residue of one specific integer at each stage, and the orbit-specificity
barrier is precisely the statement that population facts do not transfer
to this orbit---witnessed formally by
\lean{EM/Meta/OrbitBarrier.lean}{population\_does\_not\_determine\_hitting},
two walks with identical multiplier statistics, one of which hits the
death class and one of which misses it forever.

The decisive evidence is the function field model of
\S\ref{sec:function-field}.  Over $\FF_p[t]$ the Riemann Hypothesis
\emph{is} a theorem, and there the results of this paper become
unconditional---but still only for \emph{almost all} starting points.  In
the world where RH is proved, the specific-orbit statement remains open.
That is about as direct a demonstration as one could ask that RH is not
the missing ingredient.  Where RH-type input does matter here is
narrower: upgrading Dirichlet density to natural density needs a
prime-number theorem in progressions.  That is bookkeeping about density
notions, not a route to MC.

\paragraph{Why believe it true---and provable?}
\emph{True}: the case is strong.  Past the sieve gap, $\Prod{2}(n)+1$ is
automatically $q$-rough for every prime below~$q$---those all divide
$\Prod{2}(n)$---so the only remaining constraint is the single congruence
$\Prod{2}(n) \not\equiv -1 \pmod q$.  Heuristically that fails with
probability $1/q$ at each of infinitely many steps, and $\sum 1/q$
diverges.  And past the gap a hit \emph{forces} capture: that is
$\minFac$'s $0$-accessibility.  So the heuristic does not merely predict
hitting, it predicts selection.  The growth ordering of
\S\ref{sec:variants} reinforces it: order the selection rules by how fast
they grow the accumulator and one gets the same order as by how badly
they fail.

\emph{Provable}: the case is much weaker, and we will not pretend
otherwise.  Every technique that has ever worked on a Euclid-type
sequence is now proved unavailable for this one.  Even the weakest
consequences---(V), WM, RD, (C$\infty$)---are open.  On the other side,
the problem is plain $\Pi_2$ arithmetic with no whiff of independence,
the reduction chain is tight rather than foggy, and the one-horizon
criterion narrows the requirement to a single Fourier window per prime.
If a breakthrough comes we would guess expansion-type machinery---a
spectral gap in $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$---since that is the one family of
methods that has produced equidistribution for individual orbits rather
than averages.

\begin{frontierbox}
\textbf{The thesis, in one sentence.}
Mullin's conjecture is true \emph{because} $\minFac$ is structureless,
and hard for exactly the same reason.  The slow growth that gives every
prime infinitely many chances is the same fact that makes the selection
condition $\minFac(N) = q$ pure congruence data
(\S\ref{sec:anatomy-axis})---and pure congruence data is provably
insufficient.  $\mathrm{maxFac}$ is provably incomplete because
$\mathrm{maxFac}(N) = q$ hands you smoothness; $\minFac$ hands you
nothing at all.  The rule most likely to be true is the rule that gives
the least to work with.
\end{frontierbox}
