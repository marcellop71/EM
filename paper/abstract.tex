\begin{abstract}
\footnotesize
The Euclid--Mullin sequence is $a(0)=2$,
$a(n{+}1) = \text{lpf}(a(0)\cdots a(n)+1)$, with $\text{lpf}$ the least prime
factor; Mullin's Conjecture (MC, 1963) says every prime appears.  Its
running product evolves by the \emph{Euclid step}
$T(n) = n\cdot\text{lpf}(n{+}1)$ on squarefree integers, so the sequence is
the orbit of~$2$ under~$T$ and MC asks whether that orbit meets every
prime.  We present a Lean~4 formalization ($\EMLineCount$~lines,
\EMFileCount~files, zero~\texttt{sorry}) mapping what one orbit of~$T$
provably inherits from~$T$.
Two exact projections frame it, and they are not on a par: the
\emph{residue walk} $T^n(2) \bmod q$ carries MC itself, turning it into a
hitting statement on $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$ (its algebraic precondition proved
free), and holds all the upper structure; the \emph{growth constant}
$C = \lim_N \log T^N(2)/2^N$, a semiconjugacy to doubling, carries only the
floor---its vanishing is a complete invariant for $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$,
``infinitely many Euclid numbers are composite''---and MC is invisible
from it.
At the top is the machine-verified chain
$\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \mathrm{CCSB} \Rightarrow \mathrm{MC}$, where CME
(conditional multiplier equidistribution along the orbit, for a missing
prime) \emph{reformulates} MC in the language of the walk: quantified over
missing primes, so MC implies it vacuously and $\mathrm{CME}
\Leftrightarrow \mathrm{CCSB} \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{DH} \Leftrightarrow
\mathrm{MC}$ (all machine-checked); its content is the direction turning a
hitting problem into an equidistribution problem.
Nothing sits under it: weaker-looking hypotheses collapse to CME, are
walled by orbit-specificity (finite counterexamples in
$(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$), or are false---fixed-modulus equidistribution of
least prime factors is proved equivalent to a series identity that fails
by head domination.  At the bottom sits $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$,
Sylvester-tower primality: MC implies a no-infinite-prime-tower statement
for a doubly exponential sequence, which is open.  Around it: no propagating
congruence invariant blocks a prime under $\text{lpf}$, while the same class
machine-checks Cox--van der Poorten's omission of~$5$ under the largest
prime factor; a comparison class (ensemble, factor tree, two-point,
$\varepsilon$-random, function field) isolates \emph{selection} as the
difficulty, with almost-all factor-tree hitting unconditional; and
Karamata's Tauberian theorem with Mertens in progressions is formalized
along the way.  For the greedy map itself, one unconditional
theorem does clear the seed ensemble: for each \emph{fixed} prime~$q$ and
each $\varepsilon>0$ there is a finite horizon~$n$ at which the seeds
coprime to~$q$ missing~$q$ in their first~$n$ multipliers have upper
natural density at most~$\varepsilon$---no character sums, an exact CRT
selection law against a deterministic compensator bound, and nothing
claimed about the orbit of~$2$.
Simultaneity in~$q$ is a question of additivity, not of rates: for natural
density it stays open past finite sets of primes, upper density being only
finitely subadditive, while on the profinite ensemble
$\Omega = \prod_{r} \ZZ/r\ZZ$, where the same counting chain is reused and
countable additivity is available, $\mu$-almost every point captures every
prime whose coordinate it does not annihilate---a different ensemble, in
which $\NN$ is $\mu$-null, so neither statement strengthens the other.
\DEentries~dead ends are catalogued with the reason each fails.
\end{abstract}
