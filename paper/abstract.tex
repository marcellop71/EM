\begin{abstract}
The Euclid--Mullin sequence is $a(0)=2$,
$a(n{+}1) = \text{lpf}(a(0)\cdots a(n)+1)$, with $\text{lpf}$ the least
prime factor; Mullin's Conjecture (MC, 1963) says every prime appears.
The running product $a(0)\cdots a(n)$ evolves by the \emph{Euclid step}
$T(n) = n\cdot\text{lpf}(n{+}1)$ on squarefree integers (multiply the bag of
primes collected so far by the least prime factor of the product plus
one), so the sequence is the orbit of~$2$ under~$T$, and MC asks whether
that orbit meets every prime.
We present a Lean~4 formalization ($\EMLineCount$~lines,
\EMFileCount~files, zero~\texttt{sorry}) that maps what one orbit of~$T$
provably inherits from the structure of~$T$.
Two exact projections frame the map, and they are not on a par: the
\emph{residue walk} $T^n(2) \bmod q$ carries MC itself, turning it into a
hitting statement on $(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times$ (its algebraic precondition
proved free), and is where all the upper structure lives; the
\emph{growth constant} $C = \lim_N \log T^N(2)/2^N$, a semiconjugacy
$C(Tm) = 2C(m)$ to doubling, carries only the floor---its vanishing is a
complete invariant for $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$, ``infinitely many Euclid
numbers are composite''---and MC is invisible from it.
At the top of the map is the machine-verified chain
$\mathrm{CME} \Rightarrow \mathrm{CCSB} \Rightarrow \mathrm{MC}$, where
CME (conditional multiplier equidistribution along the orbit, for a
missing prime) is a \emph{reformulation} of MC in the language of the
walk: since it is quantified over missing primes, MC implies it
vacuously and $\mathrm{CME} \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{CCSB} \Leftrightarrow
\mathrm{DH} \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{MC}$ (all machine-checked); its
content is the direction that turns a hitting problem into an
equidistribution problem.  Nothing sits under it: weaker-looking
hypotheses collapse to CME, are
walled by orbit-specificity (finite counterexamples in
$(\ZZ/5\ZZ)^\times$), or are false---fixed-modulus population
equidistribution of least prime factors is proved equivalent to a series
identity that fails by head domination.  At the bottom, weak forms of MC
bottom out at $(\mathrm{C}\infty)$, Sylvester-tower primality: MC implies
a no-infinite-prime-tower statement for a doubly exponential sequence,
which is open.  Around the map: no propagating congruence
invariant blocks a prime under $\text{lpf}$, while the same class
machine-checks Cox--van der Poorten's omission of~$5$ under the largest
prime factor; a comparison class (ensemble, factor tree, two-point,
$\varepsilon$-random, function field) isolates \emph{selection} as the
difficulty, with almost-all factor-tree hitting unconditional; and
Karamata's Tauberian theorem with Mertens' theorem in progressions is
formalized along the way.  150 dead ends are catalogued with the reason each fails; every
result and open hypothesis is tagged and registered in the public
repository.
